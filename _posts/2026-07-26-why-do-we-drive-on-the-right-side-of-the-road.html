Did you know that in the Middle Ages, pilgrims on their way to Rome could see a sign instructing them to "keep left"? Long before cars and highways, people throughout Europe "drove" on the left side of the road. This had a practical reason. If an enemy or troublemaker passed, your sword arm, which for most people is your right arm, was at the ready to defend you. Medieval staircases are formed clockwise for the same reason--an advantage over the intruder.

Napoleon Bonaparte changed all that. First off, because he was left-handed. Second, because he wanted to assert his power. Third, because in his view, driving on the right was more democratic. This was because the aristocracy had priority to the left lanes of travel, wherease the peasants kept to the right. This is one reason Marie Antoinette was so easy to smell when she tried to escape the guillotine!

The areas of Europe that were conquered by Napoleon all swithced [note to Jeff: there is no spell check. Perhaps this is a good thing!] to the right, and then even countries that were not conquered began to follow suit, with the exception of the UK. The last country to switch was Sweden, and not until 1967!
